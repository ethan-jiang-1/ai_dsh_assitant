# Market sizing

An estimate is an argument with numbers. A single number is not.

## State the assumptions before estimating

Target customer, pain intensity, what they use today — each item labelled **fact** (with its source:
the principal, the knowledge room, or a past session) or **assumption** (with what would verify it).
A number with no label does not appear on the page.

## Estimate both ways

- **Bottom-up**: reachable customers × annual win rate × contract value (project and first-year
  maintenance kept separate).
- **Top-down**: the customer's annual budget pool for this class of spend × the share realistically
  winnable.

If the two estimates land more than roughly 3× apart, the assumptions are wrong — go back and fix
them. Splitting the difference is not an estimate; it is a way of hiding one.

## Qualify the single deal before it counts

Six questions, all of which must be answerable (the MEDDIC discipline): **Metrics** (which number the
customer wants to move) · **Economic buyer** (who signs) · **Decision criteria** (how they will judge
it) · **Decision process** (how they buy) · **Identified pain** (what hurts today) · **Champion** (who
inside wants this to succeed). A deal that cannot answer these is not ready — it does not go into the
numerator of any estimate, and it does not enter a pipeline as if it were revenue.

## Stress the estimate

Take the two least certain assumptions and move each ±50%. If the verdict flips, say so plainly:
this estimate cannot carry the decision. Do not pick the flattering direction.

## Verdict first

One paragraph at the top: pursue / pass / pursue under conditions (conditions listed) — plus a
one-line reason. "Nobody buys this" is a legitimate conclusion; recommend it plainly when the
evidence points there.

## One page, and where it lives

The memo fits on one page. If the principal wants it kept, `assistant_reflect save_page` with
`type: decision-memo` and `sources` pointing at the thread or memory it came from — it lands as a
draft and counts as knowledge only when the principal approves it.

## Honesty rules

- Market and pricing only. "Can we build it" and "can we deliver it" belong to other lenses — say so
  instead of answering for them.
- Customer facts come from the principal or the knowledge room. Too few customer data points is a
  finding ("sample too small to conclude"), not an obstacle to invent around.
