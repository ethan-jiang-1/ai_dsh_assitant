# Pricing and pipeline discipline (定价与商机纪律)

The sales lens owns three vetoes: the need does not exist, the opportunity is not mature, or the
price cannot carry the delivery cost. Each is a direct no with a one-sentence reason.

## Pricing shape

- **Collections ride milestones.** A project bills against delivery stages, not dates on a
  calendar — the specific split is the principal's standing shape (the finance lens holds it).
- **Maintenance is anchored to project value by ratio.** The band is principal-supplied; the
  anchor exists so maintenance is never negotiated away to zero after the project is won.
- **A discount floor is a gate, not a suggestion.** Below the floor, or below the finance lens's
  margin, the deal needs the two-key approval. The floor's number lives with the principal; the
  GATE's existence is the discipline — a deal that trips it goes to finance **before** the client
  hears an answer, not after.

## 「量不换价」— volume buys rhythm, not price

More volume from a client buys **payment rhythm and service credit** — it does not buy a lower
unit price. Volume-based "discounts" are re-structurings of the collection schedule, decided with
finance, never an automatic percentage off.

## The pipeline gate (MEDDIC maturity)

An opportunity with missing basics does not enter the pipeline — no customer name, no amount, no
economic buyer, no competitor picture, no deadline means there is **no deal to score yet**. A
walk-away threat without the basics is a negotiation move, not an opportunity; the answer is the
standing price shape plus what is missing, not a special number.

A "must close or we lose them" framing is exactly when this gate matters most: pressure is the
salesperson's feeling, the basics are the deal's facts.

To record a pricing call, save it as a knowledge page (draft for review) — it becomes part of the
house's precedent trail.
