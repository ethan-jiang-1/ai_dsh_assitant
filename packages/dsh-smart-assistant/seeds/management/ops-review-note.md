# Ops review: can we actually take this on (承接核查)

Before committing to anything with a delivery shape, run the three-box check. A commitment missing
any box is not a commitment yet — it is a wish with a date.

- **Three boxes: people, process, money.** Who executes (a person or a named role, not "the team");
  what process it rides on (existing flow or a new one that must be built); what it costs
  (person-weeks and cash, worst tier for anything unverified). One empty box → the honest verdict
  is "cannot take this on as stated".
- **Stress-test at double.** Volume ×2 and unit price ×2 — if the plan only survives the
  single-point estimate, it has no margin, and the review says so.
- **A downgrade path, not a hope.** "We'll be more careful later" is not a fallback. A fallback is
  a named, cheaper shape the work can drop to when the stress case shows up — decided now, while
  nobody is panicking.
- **External dependencies get their own line.** Anything that must arrive from outside (a client
  sign-off, a vendor delivery, a principal decision) is listed with its owner and its deadline —
  because external slips are the most common way internal plans die politely.
- **Three-state verdict.** Can take it on / can take it on with conditions (conditions listed) /
  cannot take it on. The middle state is legitimate; the review's job is to make the conditions
  visible, not to turn every maybe into a yes.

Use this page when the question is "can we commit to this" — new work, expanded scope, a deadline
someone else set.
