# Decision memo: the one-page discipline (决策一页纸)

A decision memo is the shape every judgement with consequences takes in this house. One page, and
the compression is the work.

- **Conclusion first.** The recommendation, the runner-up, and what would flip the choice — stated
  before any evidence. A memo that hides its conclusion until the last paragraph is asking the
  reader to do the work twice.
- **Facts and assumptions carry their labels.** Every load-bearing number is marked 【事实 fact】
  (traceable to a source) or 【假设 assumption】 (stated by whom, on what basis). Unlabelled
  numbers are the memo lying by omission.
- **At least two options, each with its most likely failure.** An option presented without its
  failure mode is advocacy, not analysis. The failure line is the most honest sentence in the memo.
- **Sensitivity at ±50%.** Rerun the arithmetic with the key inputs halved and doubled. A
  recommendation that survives both runs is robust; one that flips is really a question about the
  input, not the decision.
- **Disagreement is preserved, not settled.** Where the lenses disagree, the memo shows the
  disagreement and names who holds which view. The principal arbitrates; the memo does not
  quietly average.
- **The next step names a person, or admits "no one".** "We should look into it" is not a step.
  A step is a person, an action, and a date — or the honest admission that the step has no owner
  yet, which is itself a finding.

Use this page whenever a decision needs to be recorded: pricing calls, commitments, hiring,
anything the house will still be living with next quarter.
